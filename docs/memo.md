## Bottom line
Across Johnson & Johnson, Pfizer, Merck, and Eli Lilly, Lilly looks strongest on profitability and Pfizer weakest, while J&J has the thinnest liquidity. Lilly also carries the most debt relative to equity. These are one-year ratios from a simple tool, so treat them as questions to investigate, not conclusions.

## What stands out
1. **Liquidity:** J&J's current ratio (1.03) is the lowest of the four pharma companies, meaning current assets barely cover current liabilities. Merck and Lilly sit around 1.5.
2. **Profitability:** Lilly has the highest gross margin (83.0%) and operating margin (45.6%). Pfizer's net margin (12.4%) is far below the other three, which are all near 28-32%. The data does not show why, so that is the first thing to look into.
3. **Leverage:** Lilly's debt-to-equity is 1.60, versus 0.59 for J&J. Lilly's was 2.36 in fiscal 2024, so it moved a lot in one year.
4. **ROE:** Lilly's 77.8% is high partly because ROE is divided by a small equity base. High debt-to-equity and high ROE together deserve a closer look before reading that as pure strength.

## Where the bank fits
JPMorgan is included as a contrast case. Current ratio, gross margin, and debt-to-equity are not meaningful for a bank, so only net margin (31.3%) and ROE (15.7%) are shown. Its ROE sits well below the strongest pharma companies in this sample, but the businesses are too different for that to be a ranking.

## Limits
- **Operating margin is a proxy** (gross profit - R&D - SG&A) for every company, because none of them tag operating income. It excludes items like acquired in-process R&D and restructuring.
- **ROE uses year-end equity,** not average equity, so it will not match company-reported ROE.
- **One-time items distort single years.** J&J's 2025 net earnings include about $7.2B of other income, and its 2023 net earnings include $21.8B from discontinued operations.
- **Only J&J was verified** line by line against its 10-K (8 of 8 matched). The other four companies were not manually checked.
- **Debt is defined as** loans and notes payable plus long-term debt, which excludes items such as leases.
- Five companies and one year are not a statistical sample, and ratios alone cannot explain why a company looks the way it does.

## Suggested next steps
Verify Pfizer's, Merck's, and Lilly's inputs against their 10-Ks, then look at Pfizer's net margin and Lilly's equity base and debt over several years.
